How this works

Every item here is a passage from the book that belongs to somebody else. For each one there are two questions: does it need permission at all, and if so, where has that got to.

Two words that are easy to mix up, and the difference matters here.

Public domain means copyright has expired and anyone may use the work freely. It is decided by age and by when the author died. Shakespeare, the King James Bible and Emily Dickinson are all public domain, and there is nothing to ask.

But an old work can still carry a new copyright. Dante died in 1321 and Catullus two thousand years ago — yet the English translation quoted in this book is a separate, recent work with its own copyright. This catches people out constantly, and roughly a sixth of the list is here for exactly that reason. So when marking something public domain, please say why in the box provided: published before 1930, author died in such a year, and so on.

There is a third idea, fair use, which sometimes permits short quotation without asking. It is a defence argued case by case rather than a category a passage belongs to, so it is deliberately not one of the options here. If counsel wants to rely on it for any item, the note field is the place to record that.

What to do: work through the groups, most serious first. For each item set what it is, what we want to do, who writes the letter if one is needed, and where it has got to. Add a note for anything worth remembering — who you spoke to, what they said.

Everything saves by itself and everyone with the link sees the same page, so Elliott, Rebecca and counsel are always looking at the same state. Your name is stamped on whatever you change.
