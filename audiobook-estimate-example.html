That range is not hedging. Final manuscript length, reading pace, and how much editing a narrator needs are genuinely unknown until the first session, so the estimate stays a range until there is real information to narrow it. Billing is based on actual hours worked - if the sessions go faster than expected, you pay less.

Why this is billed hourly, not per finished hour

Most audiobook production is quoted “per finished hour” — a fixed price for each hour of completed audio. That model exists to protect the studio: it locks in a price regardless of how long the work actually takes.

The trade-off is that a fixed price has to assume the worst case. If you are a well-prepared narrator who nails long takes, a per-finished-hour quote charges you for problems you did not cause. Billing actual hours means preparation pays you back. On a typical book this lands around $225–$315 per finished hour — comparable to standard rates — but the number moves with the real work rather than a worst-case guess.

What moves the number

Finished length

The single biggest factor. Manuscript word count is the best early predictor of total hours.

Narrator experience

A first-time author narrating their own book needs more takes and more editing than a seasoned voice actor. Both are welcome; they cost differently.

Reading pace

Pace determines how many studio hours a given word count turns into, and it varies more between people than most expect.

Edit depth

Light cleanup versus tight breath and pause work is a real difference in hours, and it is a choice you get to make.

In-studio or on location

Recording here has no setup cost. Building a treated space at your home or office adds transport, setup, and breakdown time.

Additional narrators

Integrating technically sound outside recordings is straightforward. Remote direction or repairing poor source audio is extra.

What an estimate deliberately leaves out

Every estimate names what is not covered, so nothing arrives as a surprise later. On a typical audiobook that includes:

  • Proof-listening. A full word-for-word comparison against the manuscript is its own substantial job. Who does it — you, a proofer, or this studio — gets decided during scoping, not assumed.
  • Distribution upload and metadata. Final files are prepared to match your chosen platform's spec. Actually uploading the book and entering its metadata is quoted separately.
  • Rights clearance. Permission for quoted poems, lyrics, or third-party text stays with the author. This studio does not clear rights.
  • Significant repair of outside audio. Recordings from another narrator get integrated if they are technically sound. Rescuing bad source material is additional time.

How payment works

A deposit reserves your recording window. Recording and editing hours are billed at milestones as the work progresses, and the final balance is due before the completed master files are delivered. For a project this size, nobody pays for the whole book upfront and nobody waits until the end to get paid.

Common questions about audiobook recording

How much does it cost to record an audiobook in San Francisco?

At Studio San Francisco a full-length audiobook typically runs $3,000-$4,230. That reflects roughly 30-40 hours of recording at $60/hr, 20-30 hours of editing and post-production at $45/hr, and 5-8 hours of mobile setup and removal at $60/hr when recording on location. Work is billed at actual hours, not a flat package, and every project is scoped before it starts.

Is audiobook work priced per finished hour?

No. Studio San Francisco bills actual hours worked - $60/hr with the client in the room and $45/hr for solo editing - rather than a per-finished-hour rate. On a typical book this works out to roughly $225-$315 per finished hour, but the client only pays for time actually spent, so a well-prepared narrator costs less rather than subsidising a fixed price.

Can an author narrate their own audiobook?

Yes. Authors narrating their own books are welcome and are guided through pacing, retakes, and delivery by the engineer during the session. First-time narrators typically need more recording and editing hours than experienced voice talent, which is why the estimate is a range rather than a fixed price.

What is not included in an audiobook estimate?

Full word-for-word proof-listening against the manuscript, distribution platform upload and metadata entry, remote direction of additional narrators, and rights clearance for quoted material are quoted separately or remain the author's responsibility. These are named explicitly in every estimate rather than left ambiguous.

Can you record an audiobook at my home or office?

Yes. Studio San Francisco can build a temporary treated recording setup at a client's location in the San Francisco Bay Area, which is often the right call for long-form audiobook work. Setup and removal time is quoted as its own line item so it is visible rather than buried in the hourly rate.
